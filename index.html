The Law of Sines is a simple rule in geometry that relates the lengths of the sides of a triangle to the sines of its angles. The best part? It works for any triangle, not just right-angled ones. The Formula
Imagine a triangle with sides \(a\), \(b\), and \(c\), and the angles opposite to those sides named \(A\), \(B\), and \(C\). The law states that the ratio of the length of a side to the sine of its opposite angle is always equal for all three sides: \(\frac{a}{\sin (A)}=\frac{b}{\sin (B)}=\frac{c}{\sin (C)}\) You can also flip it upside down if you are looking for an angle: \(\frac{\sin (A)}{a}=\frac{\sin (B)}{b}=\frac{\sin (C)}{c}\) When to Use It You can use this law to find a missing side or angle when you already know a "matching pair" (a side and its opposite angle) plus one other piece of information. It is perfect for:
• ASA (Angle-Side-Angle): You know two angles and the side between them.
• AAS (Angle-Angle-Side): You know two angles and a side outside them.
• SSA (Side-Side-Angle): You know two sides and an angle outside them.
